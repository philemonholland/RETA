# Author’s statement, acknowledgements and thanks

I am making this research fully open and public because of the urgency created by the existential threat posed by currently unaligned artificial intelligence. It is available to be examined, challenged and developed collaboratively. The model remains **UNALIGNED and actively evolving**.

[RETA — Recursive Event–Trajectory Alignment](https://www.hortrame.com/alignment/reta/) is the direct evolution of a personal body of research that long predates its present formulation as an AI-alignment framework. Before these online releases, my underlying manuscripts and my own synthesis had never been published, and I had never attempted to publish them. I left my master’s research in education at the Université de Sherbrooke before completing it. I also did not complete my later undergraduate studies in robotics engineering. Neither the present model nor the proposed mathematical extension has received independent expert validation or peer review.

## Information literacy and the evaluation of knowledge

The earlier phase grew out of three years of work spanning 2013–2016. It concerned information literacy, the evaluation of information and its sources, misinformation in education, educational neuromyths, and the gap between confidence and demonstrated competence. The aim was to understand how people could make better decisions about information, recognize the limits of their understanding and improve the processes by which they learn. That work became an unfinished, unpublished master’s manuscript.

I worked personally with [Vincent Grenon](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/recherche/fr/specialistes/details/vincent.grenon) and [François Larose](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/education/nous-joindre/professeurs-associes) for nearly a decade. Both were part of Sherbrooke’s Faculty of Education and supervised my research. Grenon is a professor whose work includes educational technology and quantitative methods; Larose is an affiliated professor in education. I owe much of my grounding in information literacy—and the social, intellectual, ethical and scientific rigor of my epistemological process—to their past support and influence.

My information-literacy model was my own synthesis of earlier frameworks, particularly those associated with SCONUL, ACRL, ANZIIL, NILIS/Empowering 8, IFLA and CILIP. It was never peer reviewed. It organized the recognition of information needs, access, critical evaluation, organization, use and communication as an interconnected process. Metacognition operated across and beyond those activities, while ethics and law framed the whole process. That structure is an important ancestor of the present model’s recursive evaluation, attention to uncertainty and insistence on revising the process of judgment itself.

## From misinformation to the deliberate manipulation of knowledge

The two-year phase spanning 2017–2018 took the same questions into darker territory. The later manuscript examined propaganda, deliberate disinformation, information-psychological warfare, reflexive control, strategic deception, plausible deniability and the laundering of information. It also examined conspiracy narratives, automated and semi-automated amplification, the exploitation of social-media algorithms, political polarization, authoritarian populism, fear, intolerance, the erosion of trust and the fragility of democratic institutions. These were extensions of the research I had begun in 2015, not a completed or published master’s thesis.

The connection to the current model is substantive: information changes the conditions under which people interpret the world and act. An evaluation therefore needs to consider the source, the surrounding incentives and relationships, the effects on agency, and the consequences that continue after a message has been received. The manuscripts also drew on the tradition of propaganda analysis associated with the Institute for Propaganda Analysis, Leonard Doob and Jacques Ellul; research on misinformation and its persistence; cognitive accounts of threat appraisal; and Christian Welzel’s work on human empowerment and emancipative values. These strands helped connect epistemic integrity with agency and responsibility.

There were negative influences as well. Observing Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump and other men throughout history who, in my judgment, wielded power they did not deserve helped push this inquiry toward the deliberate corruption of shared knowledge. I do not thank them. Their influence belongs in the history of the questions that made this work necessary to me.

## Bayesian reasoning and reflexive models

The Bayesian influence grew partly from work I did for [François Claveau](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/philosophie/departement/personnel/personnel-enseignant/francois-claveau), a professor of philosophy whose research includes social and formal epistemology. From September 2016 to January 2017, I worked with the **Canada Research Chair in Practical Epistemology**, developing an interactive web-based tool for learning Bayes’ theorem. The university’s [announcement of the research team](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/flsh/actualites/nouvelles/details/32165) names me and describes the wider assignment of teaching critical thinking through web tools. This work contributed to the present model’s treatment of evidence, uncertainty and revision. I thank François for his past support and intellectual influence; he has not reviewed or endorsed this model.

The emotional-reflexive strand draws on Sergey Tarasenko’s *Applications of the Reflexive Game Theory: Advanced Topics* (2016) and his paper [*Emotionally Colorful Reflexive Games*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.0820), submitted in 2010. His work connects reflexive game theory with the Pleasure–Arousal–Dominance model of emotion and considers relationships among human and robotic agents. It builds on the mathematical psychologist Vladimir A. Lefebvre’s foundational work on reflexive models of choice and interaction; Tarasenko describes nearly five years of collaboration with Lefebvre in the book. I never met or spoke with Lefebvre.

I thank Sergey specifically for his kindness in sending me the PDFs of his book and article. That exchange was the extent of our contact. We did not collaborate, and he has not seen, reviewed or approved this work. The intellectual influence comes from the material he shared, especially its treatment of how agents represent themselves, others and the situations in which decisions are made.

## Experimental origins and later formalization

A smaller but significant mathematical strand comes from experimental discoveries I made in 2024 while implementing multi-agent control on a physical robot swarm. During that internship, supervised by [Maude Blondin](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/genie-electrique-informatique/departement/corps-professoral), an assistant professor working on control, multi-agent systems and optimization, I built a reusable experimental platform and implemented an algorithm associated with doctoral researcher **Shokoufeh Naderi**. The related source work is identified in [Naderi, Blondin and Roy’s paper on consensus with communication delays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16523).

The proposed extension grew from my discoveries during that implementation work. I later gave Astra the complete historical archive of my internship code, and the full formalization was developed in collaboration with Astra. The present theorem, simulation, full and compact articles, and accompanying research materials were prepared and uploaded on **September 7, 2026**. They are publicly available at [HORTRAME Swarm](https://www.hortrame.com/swarm/) for anyone who wishes to examine them. The new formalization has not been validated by independent experts, and the study has not been peer reviewed. Maude’s supervision concerned the internship; she has not seen or approved the new theorem. I thank her for the rigor and support she brought to that earlier work. The later formalization must not be confused with the algorithm tested during the internship, nor taken as proof of the broader AI-alignment framework.

In 2025, I began my first experiments explicitly directed at ethical AI alignment: a very simplified implementation of the **Five Vows vectorial framework**. That ethical model remained unfinished, but it became an important part of the present project. Its development overlapped with my internship under [Fabien Alibart](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/ln2/a-propos/equipe/fabien-alibart), a CNRS researcher and affiliated professor at Sherbrooke working on neuromorphic devices and circuits. My internship involved electrical characterization and measurement automation for memristors intended for analog neural networks—hardware in which physical devices can perform memory-like and synapse-like functions.

I thank Fabien for his support and scientific influence during that work. The ethical AI model was a separate undertaking. I sense a connection between that reflection and neuromorphic research, but I have not established one. The internship is not evidence for the ethical model, and Fabien has not reviewed or endorsed it.

## Evolution and complex systems

The model’s treatment of evolution and complex systems owes a substantial intellectual debt to [Alan Cohen](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/profile/alan-cohen-phd), an associate professor of environmental health sciences at Columbia whose research connects physiology, aging, ecology and evolution. Alan has been a close personal friend and mentor for a decade. We have never worked together professionally. I owe him extensive thanks for his friendship, past support and the rigor his influence has brought to my thinking.

The ideas I draw from this body of work include regulation distributed across interacting systems; multidimensional state rather than a single measure of health or success; context-dependent responses to perturbation; and evolution constrained by architecture, history and trade-offs. The work of his mentor, the evolutionary ecologist **Robert E. Ricklefs**, whom I never met, is part of this intellectual lineage. These influences help explain the present model’s overlapping system boundaries, multiple diagnostic dimensions and attention to trajectories rather than isolated scores. Their use here is a methodological extension into a new setting, not a claim that biological research validates an AI ethics model.

There is also practical continuity with my work building automation and operational tools: approval processes, audit trails, human review, recovery procedures, and verification that an intended action actually produced its recorded effect. Those concerns recur in the model’s event histories and prospective revision rules. The current formalization also draws on the literature on causal ordering and logical clocks, multilayer networks and hybrid dynamical systems, identified in the [references](https://www.hortrame.com/alignment/references/).
